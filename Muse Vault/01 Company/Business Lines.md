# Business Lines

Three pillars, one brand. Listed in the order Abdullah thinks about them, which is not the order money arrives in.

| # | Pillar | Folder | What it is for | Revenue shape |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Product Ventures | [[Ventures]] | The long game. Where company value accumulates | Nothing for a while, then possibly a lot |
| 2 | Brand and Marketing | [[Marketing]] | Audience, reputation, reach, and what those unlock | No direct revenue. Makes the other two cheaper |
| 3 | Client Services | [[Studio]] | Pays for everything at the start | Project based, lumpy |

## 1. Product Ventures

A portfolio of localised versions of products that already work somewhere else. Ship a real MVP, publish it, watch what happens, and put more behind whichever one shows signs of life. See [[Product Philosophy]].

The logic is portfolio logic. One hit pays for every miss, so the cost of each attempt matters more than the odds on any single one.

The design lead owns this pillar and keeps it as their main focus, so it does not stop the moment a client appears. See [[Business Lines#Who owns what]].

## 2. Brand and Marketing

Building an audience that knows who we are and what we are good at, through content that is useful and does not sound like a company talking.

This pillar produces no invoices, which is exactly why it has to be named as a pillar rather than treated as something to do when there is time. What it buys is set out in [[Influence Strategy]]. Short version: inbound client work, a launch audience for our own products, a way into the entities that back startups here, and public standing for the people who work here.

## 3. Client Services

Consulting and delivery for people and companies who need product, design, development, branding, marketing or graphics work. Full menu in [[Service Catalogue]], process in [[Engagement Lifecycle]].

This is the main source of income at the beginning and there is nothing embarrassing about that. It funds the other two pillars, teaches us what businesses here actually struggle with, and produces the case studies that make the next client easier to win.

## How the pillars feed each other

The connections are the whole reason to run three things at once.

- Client work funds Ventures and Marketing, and surfaces real problems worth building products around
- Marketing means client work arrives inbound rather than by chasing, which raises what we can charge and lets us say no
- Marketing gives every venture launch a starting audience instead of a cold App Store listing
- Ventures prove we can ship, which is what makes a serious client trust us with their product
- The work itself is the content. Building in public costs almost nothing extra when someone is already filming

## Who owns what

Each pillar has one person responsible for it. Everyone else supports across all three. Main focus is what someone is accountable for, not the only thing they touch.

| Pillar | Responsible | Supported by |
|---|---|---|
| [[Ventures]] | Design lead. Design and product direction | Technical lead, when an idea needs turning into something buildable. Founder on direction and the traction gate |
| [[Marketing]] | Marketing lead, see [[Marketing Lead Brief]] | Graphic design and production help, plus the rest of the team appearing in and contributing content |
| [[Studio]] | Founder. Client relationships and commercials | Technical lead on everything technical, plus client delivery support and whoever else the engagement needs |

Full detail in [[Roles]].

### Support is the normal state, not an exception

Nobody works alone on their pillar, and asking for help is expected rather than a sign the person is struggling.

- **Marketing gets whatever it needs.** Graphic designers, production help, people on camera, technical explanations from whoever built the thing. A marketing lead with no supporting hands produces a quiet account
- **Ventures gets technical support.** Design ideas have to become something buildable, and that means someone technical in the room early, throwing ideas around rather than receiving a finished handover
- **Client work draws on everyone.** Whatever the engagement needs. The founder fronts it, the technical lead carries the build, and other people come in as the work requires

### Why owners at all, if everyone helps

Because unowned work does not happen. Three people helping with marketing and nobody accountable for it produces exactly what it sounds like. The owner decides, keeps the notes current, and is the person to ask. Everyone else makes their job possible.

## Capacity rule

Decided, see [[Decision Log]].

**Client work has first call on shared capacity.** When there is an active engagement, it wins. That is what pays salaries.

**When there is no active client demand, the default work is Ventures and Marketing.** Not admin, not waiting. There is always something in those two pillars to move forward, and the bench is where they get built.

**Owners keep their main focus through a busy period.** The point of naming an owner per pillar is that Ventures and Marketing do not go to zero the moment an engagement lands. Support flows to client work, focus does not.

**Client work that is itself marketing or branding is normal.** If a client needs brand or social work, the same people are doing both and our own accounts go quieter that week. Acceptable, as long as it is a decision rather than a drift.

## The honest risk in that rule

Client work will always feel more urgent than a venture, because someone is waiting and there is an invoice at the end. "Ventures happen on the bench" works only if the bench actually exists.

The failure mode is six straight months of full client load, at the end of which nothing of our own has shipped and the company is an agency that talks about products. The check against it is in [[Weekly Review]]: write down where the week actually went. Three months of no venture progress means either turning work down or hiring.

If that happens, revisit this rule and consider funding a fixed share of venture time out of client revenue instead. See [[Money#Funding the other two pillars]].
