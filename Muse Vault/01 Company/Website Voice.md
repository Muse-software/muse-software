# Website Voice

The third register. [[Brand Voice]] defines two, social and documents, and the website is neither. Decided 2026-08-01, see [[Decision Log]].

## Why a third register

The social register is warm, personal and close to people. On a page selling six figure engagements it reads as small. The document register is precise and calm, which is right in a proposal someone already asked for and too flat on a page that has to make a stranger act.

The website has one scroll to persuade someone who has never heard of us. That is a different job from both, so it gets its own rules rather than borrowing the wrong ones.

## The register in one line

Direct and confident. An operator talking, not a consultant presenting.

The reference point is Tenex, and Abdullah's read of why it works is the right one: nothing on the page is filler and every line has a point of view. Take that. Do not take their specifics, which are true for them and not for us, see [[#We do not claim what is not decided]].

## What carries over from Brand Voice

Unchanged. These already apply to both existing registers and they apply here too.

- No inflation. We are not the leading, premier or cutting edge anything
- Specific beats impressive
- Short sentences
- The banned phrase list in [[Brand Voice#Banned phrases]]
- Straight quotes only, no em dashes, no en dashes

The two that break most often on a website are inflation and specificity, because bold type makes a vague claim feel like a strong one. It is not one. It is the corporate register in a bigger font.

## What is different here

- **Distance is professional.** No first person storytelling, no named people in body copy, no behind the scenes. That is the social register and it does not belong here
- **Second person.** The page is about the reader's problem, not our history
- **Headlines carry the argument, body carries the proof.** A headline states a position. The paragraph under it has to earn it with something checkable
- **Confidence is allowed, performance is not.** State what we do plainly. No warmth theatre, no exclamation marks, no announcements
- **Every claim is either specific or cut.** There is no third option

## The specificity test

Read any sentence and ask one question: could a competitor put this on their site unchanged?

If yes, it is filler. Cut it, or rewrite it into something only we can say.

| Fails | Passes |
|---|---|
| Win the next decade | No 6 month diagnostics. No 200 slide presentations |
| Stay on the right side of history | Every engagement ends with something running in production, not a slide deck |
| We deliver exceptional digital experiences | Nothing ships that we would not use ourselves, see [[Quality Bar]] |

The failing column is not too corporate. It is too empty. That is the actual diagnosis and it is what makes the fix concrete.

## We do not claim what is not decided

The most important rule in this note, and the real risk in modelling the site on a studio we admire.

Their sharp lines are sharp because they are commitments that company has actually made. Copied across, they become promises we have not decided and cannot keep. That is the same failure as a fake chart, just harder to spot.

| Not allowed on the site until it is decided | Blocked by |
|---|---|
| Any pricing, billing or estimation model | [[Scoping & Pricing]], nothing decided |
| Client names, logos, quotes or results | No delivered client work yet, see [[Studio]] |
| Team size, roles or staffing we cannot actually field | [[Roles]] |
| A publishing history we do not have | [[Website#The content history does not exist]] |
| Arabic and RTL capability | Shown, not claimed, see below |

Same standard as [[Product Philosophy#Non negotiables]]. Real data only, applied to copy.

When a decision lands, the copy can follow it. Not before, and not in the other order.

## Arabic is shown, not claimed

Decided 2026-08-01. Saying we do Arabic properly reads as weak. Every studio here claims it, and to a buyer here it is table stakes rather than a differentiator. Claiming it out loud signals that we think it is hard.

The claim gets made by the site being genuinely bilingual and native in both scripts, not by a sentence about localisation. This is [[Positioning#How we prove it rather than claim it]] applied literally: demonstrated through the work, not through adjectives.

Until that is built, the site says nothing about it. See [[Localization Playbook]] and [[Website#Open questions]].

## The pillars are internal

Decided 2026-08-01. [[Business Lines]] is how we divide responsibility internally. It is not how we describe ourselves to anyone outside, and Ventures, Marketing and Studio never appear as public language.

There is an externally useful version of Ventures, but it is proof rather than structure. A product we shipped is evidence we can build, which is worth more on the site than any testimonial. Worth its own space when one exists. Nothing to say about it before then, see [[Ventures]].

## Applied

The shape of a section, in order:

1. **Headline.** A position, stated. Short. It should be arguable, which is how you know it says something
2. **Body.** What we actually do about it, specific enough that a reader could check it
3. **Proof, where we have it.** A number, a named constraint, something real. Where we have none, say less rather than filling the space
4. **One action.** A verb, and a single thing to do
