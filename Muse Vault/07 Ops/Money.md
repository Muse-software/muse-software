# Money

Nothing here is filled in yet. The questions are what matter.

## The three pillars have three different shapes

| Pillar | Revenue shape | Cash timing | Main cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[Studio]] | Project based, lumpy | Deposit then milestones | People's time |
| [[Marketing]] | None directly | Cost only, for a long time | People's time, and it is the easiest to underestimate |
| [[Ventures]] | Nothing for a while, then possibly a lot | Negative for a long time | Everything |

Client work pays for the other two. Knowing which pillar each riyal came from and went to is the difference between a business and a busy year.

Marketing is worth costing honestly rather than treating as free because it produces no invoice. It consumes real time and it is the pillar most likely to be quietly abandoned once that becomes visible, see [[Business Lines#The honest risk in that rule]].

## Numbers to establish

- [ ] Monthly fixed cost of running the company, everything included
- [ ] Blended cost per person week, which is the floor under any quote
- [ ] Minimum engagement value, see [[Scoping & Pricing]]
- [ ] Runway, in months, on current cash
- [ ] What a venture attempt actually costs, from shaping to published MVP. This is the key number in the portfolio model, since the whole approach depends on each attempt being cheap
- [ ] Client revenue level at which a second person focused on Ventures becomes affordable

## Track from the start

Simple beats sophisticated. Monthly, in one place:

- Revenue by pillar and by client
- Costs, fixed and variable
- Cash in the bank
- Outstanding invoices and how old they are
- Money and time spent on ventures, so the cost per attempt is knowable

## Habits worth having early

**Invoice the day the milestone is met.** Late invoicing is self inflicted cash flow trouble.

**Chase politely and immediately.** An invoice unpaid at thirty days becomes an invoice unpaid at ninety. Ask early, warmly, and in writing.

**Deposit before work starts.** No exceptions, including for friends. Especially for friends.

**Keep personal and company money completely separate,** from day one. Untangling it later is expensive and looks bad to anyone doing diligence.

**Set aside VAT and tax as it accrues,** rather than discovering the obligation at filing.

## Funding the other two pillars

Ventures and Marketing produce no revenue for a long time and both cost real money and time. Client work covers it, which is the point of [[Business Lines#Capacity rule]].

That rule funds them with leftover capacity rather than with a budget, which works while the bench exists and stops working the moment it does not. If venture progress stalls for a quarter, pick one of these and write it down:

- A fixed percentage of client revenue reserved for venture and marketing time, protected the way a client deadline is protected
- A fixed monthly budget for both, regardless of how client work is going
- A second person whose main focus is protected, so the protection is a role rather than a percentage. Same logic as the design lead on Ventures, and the version most likely to survive contact with a busy month
- External funding, which changes the shape of the company and is a separate conversation

The discipline problem is the same in every version: without a rule, the answer defaults to whatever is left over, which is nothing.
