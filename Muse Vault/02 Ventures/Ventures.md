# Ventures

Pillar one. Our own products, and where company value accumulates. The approach is in [[Product Philosophy]]: take something already proven elsewhere, build the version that fits here, publish it, and back whichever one shows signs of life.

Owned by the design lead, whose main focus stays here so this pillar does not stop every time an engagement lands. Technical support comes in early rather than at handover, see [[Business Lines#Who owns what]].

## First real venture, decided

**V-004, Executive Assistant / Employee Hub.** An AI executive assistant for Saudi professionals and small teams: one person, one AI colleague that delegates to specialist agents behind the scenes, fully personalized, Saudi data hosted, not gated by Microsoft or Azure. Built on the Hermes agent framework. Full venture card in `Venture Card - Executive Assistant.docx`, logo pack in `Muse_Venture_Executive_Assistant_Logo_Pack.zip`.

This is inspired by a similar internal product Lean (Abdullah's day job) is building, the Virtual Employee Hub. Muse's version is not a copy: same shape of idea (one assistant, many specialist agents behind it), different market, different implementation, different visual identity. Read [[Adaptation Boundary Research]] before finalizing any UI, copy, or interaction pattern, since this is the venture where the adaptation boundary question matters most directly.

Traction gate and metric to be set from [[Traction and Metrics Research]] before MVP ships, not after.

**Spread.** One product per domain, not several products in one domain. Confirmed by Abdullah 2026-08-07. The three original fitness candidates below were one conversation's illustration of localisation depth, not a chosen wedge, and stay as reference examples, not a live shortlist.

## Portfolio logic

The thing to keep in mind when any single venture looks disappointing.

We are not trying to pick the winner up front. We are trying to take enough good shots cheaply enough that one of them lands. That means the cost of an attempt is the number to manage, not the confidence behind it.

Consequences worth accepting deliberately:

- **Most of these will not work.** That is the model working, not failing
- **Small scope, real quality.** MVP means narrow, not rough. A rough MVP fails for reasons that tell you nothing, which wastes the attempt, see [[Quality Bar]]
- **Publish properly.** An MVP nobody can find has not been tested. Launch effort is part of the cost of the attempt, see [[Influence Strategy#2. Distribution for our own products]]
- **Decide with the number.** Kill or invest at the traction gate, not on feeling, see [[Venture Pipeline#Traction gate]]

## What we build

Consumer mobile apps, subscription and freemium products, AI assisted products, and internal tools that later become commercial products.

## Focus rule

Several ideas at Idea stage is healthy and costs nothing. Several in Build at once is not, at current size. One in Build, one being shaped behind it, is the realistic maximum until there are more people.

Shipping sequentially is not the same as thinking sequentially. Keep the pipeline full.

## Wedge or spread

A decision to make once the candidate list is wide enough to make it properly.

**A wedge** means several products in one domain, sharing an audience, a brand, content and data, where each launch helps the next. Much stronger than unrelated apps, and it compounds with [[Marketing]] because one audience serves everything.

**A spread** means taking the best candidate in whatever category it appears in, and starting from zero on audience every time. More freedom, more expensive per launch.

The three current candidates would happen to form a wedge, but that is because of how the conversation went, not because the domain was chosen. Do not let an accident become a strategy in either direction. See [[Open Questions#4. Which category we start in]].
