# Venture Pipeline

Five stages. Built around getting to a published MVP fast, then letting the market decide, see [[Product Philosophy#Not a research project]].

| Stage | Question it answers | Exit condition |
|---|---|---|
| **Idea** | Is this worth building | Written up and run through [[Idea Scoring]] |
| **Shape** | What is the smallest version worth publishing | Scope written down, and out of scope written down |
| **Build** | Can we ship it at our quality bar | Passes [[Quality Bar]] and [[Launch Checklist]] |
| **Live** | Is anyone using it | Instrumented, published, promoted |
| **Invest or Move On** | Does it deserve more of our time | Explicit decision at the traction gate, recorded in [[Decision Log]] |

There is no separate validation stage. That is deliberate, and it is the main way this pipeline differs from the standard advice.

## Idea

Cheap. Write the note, run the quick filter, move on. Most ideas should stop here and that is the point.

## What replaces a validation phase

We are not asking whether people want this kind of product. Millions already pay for the original, so the expensive question is answered. What we do need, and it takes days rather than months:

- **Use the reference product seriously.** A week as the actual user. This is the real research, see [[Research#How to do a teardown properly]]
- **Read its one star reviews.** The complaints about a good product are where the openings are
- **Check what already exists here.** If there is a strong Arabic first competitor already winning, that changes the idea. Usually there is not, which is the whole opportunity
- **Try the global product as a local user for one day.** Use it exactly as someone here would, in a normal local day, and screenshot every place it fails. That document is both the brief and the launch content
- **Talk to a handful of real people if they are easy to reach.** Useful, not a gate. Five conversations in a week, not thirty over two months

Kill it at this point only for a hard reason: someone here is already doing it well, we cannot build it, or there is no route to reaching users at all. "People might not want it" is not a hard reason, because that is what publishing is for.

## Shape

Write the smallest publishable version and, more importantly, write what is not in it. An unwritten out of scope list is how a two month build becomes eight.

- Core loop in one sentence
- Screens, listed
- Explicitly out of v1
- The one or two local hooks that make this ours rather than a translation, see [[Product Philosophy#Localisation is the product, not a translation layer]]
- Arabic and RTL from the first screen, never retrofitted
- What data we need and where it comes from. No fake data, ever
- Events to instrument, see [[Measurement#Instrument before launch]]

Rule of thumb: if the shaped scope is more than about two months of work, it is not an MVP yet. Cut again.

## Build

One venture in Build at a time at current size. Ship in slices that are individually usable.

Small scope, full quality. Everything goes through [[Quality Bar]]. Publishing something broken teaches us nothing about the idea, which wastes the attempt.

The build is also content. Film it as it happens, see [[Content Engine]].

## Live

Launch is the middle of the process, not the end.

- Instrumented before launch, or the first month of data is gone
- Published properly. Store listing, screenshots, Arabic and English, a real launch push through our own channels
- Someone is watching reviews and support messages daily in the first two weeks

## Traction gate

The decision that replaces months of upfront validation. Set the review date at launch and put it in the calendar.

At the gate, one of three outcomes, recorded in [[Decision Log]]:

**Invest.** Real signs of life. More people, more money, a proper roadmap.

**One more push.** Something specific is broken and we know what. One fix, one new review date. Only once, otherwise this becomes a way of never deciding.

**Move on.** Leave it live if it costs nothing to run, stop spending attention on it, write down what was learned, and start the next one. Not a failure, this is the model.

The bar itself is not set yet, and it needs to be before V-001 launches. See [[Open Questions#3. What counts as traction]]. Setting it after seeing the numbers is how a dead product survives for a year.

Two rules that hold regardless of the number:

- Decide with the metric in front of you, see [[Measurement]]
- Retention over installs. Installs measure the launch push, retention measures the product
