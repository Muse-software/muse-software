# Engagement Lifecycle

Seven stages from first contact to a client who comes back. Each one has a thing that must exist before moving on.

## 1. First conversation

Goal is to understand the problem, not to pitch. Listen for whether this is a partner relationship or a vendor request, see [[Studio#Staying a product company while doing client work]].

Questions worth asking every time:

- What made you start looking for help now
- Who uses this, and how do you know what they need
- What have you already tried
- Who decides, and who can stop it
- What does success look like in six months

Red flags: no access to users, a deadline set before scope, a fixed price expectation before the problem is defined, five agencies pitching.

Output: a note in `03 Studio/Clients` from [[Client]].

## 2. Scope and proposal

Sized and priced per [[Scoping & Pricing]]. The proposal states the problem in their words, the approach, what is included, what is explicitly not, timeline, price and payment terms. Out of scope is the most valuable section.

Output: proposal PDF in house style. Never a spreadsheet of hours.

## 3. Agreement

Contract signed, deposit received before work starts. No exceptions, including for people you like. Especially for people you like.

Checklist:

- [ ] Scope document attached to the contract, not just referenced
- [ ] Payment schedule with dates
- [ ] IP ownership stated. Who owns the code, the designs, and whether we can show it publicly
- [ ] Change process defined, so scope changes have a price rather than an argument
- [ ] Named point of contact on their side with authority to approve

## 4. Discovery

Understand before designing. Users, existing product, data, constraints, whatever they have that we have not seen.

Output: a written summary of what we found, shared with them. This is where a client first feels the difference between us and an agency.

## 5. Design and build

Slices, shown often. Nothing goes into build until Arabic and RTL are handled in the design, see [[Localization Playbook]].

- Weekly or fortnightly review with the client, always with something working in front of them
- Decisions recorded in the client note, with dates. Six months later this is what protects everyone
- Everything checked against [[Quality Bar]]

## 6. Launch

Run [[Launch Checklist]]. Do not launch on a Thursday afternoon, since the local weekend means nobody is there when it breaks.

Handover includes: access and credentials transferred properly, documentation of what was built, and a walkthrough with whoever maintains it.

## 7. After

The step almost everyone skips, and where the next project comes from.

- [ ] Case study written while it is fresh, with permission, see [[Studio#Case studies]]
- [ ] Anything postable pulled out for [[Content Engine]], within whatever permission was agreed
- [ ] Internal retrospective, what to change next time, written into [[Playbooks]]
- [ ] Check in at thirty days with real usage numbers
- [ ] Anything reusable extracted into a playbook, or noted in [[Productised Solutions]] if three clients would want it
- [ ] Ask for the introduction. It never comes unprompted
