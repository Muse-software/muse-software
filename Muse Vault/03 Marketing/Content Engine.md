# Content Engine

What we post, how it gets made, and how it keeps going when the studio gets busy.

Voice is [[Brand Voice#The public voice]]. Close to people. If a caption could have been written by any company, rewrite it.

## Content pillars

Five kinds of thing. A healthy week has more than one kind in it.

### 1. Useful craft content

Tips, techniques, breakdowns, short tutorials. The thing someone saves and sends to a colleague.

- A design or code technique explained in under a minute
- Before and after of a real screen, with the reasoning
- Common mistake, why it happens, what to do instead
- Arabic and RTL specifics that almost everyone gets wrong. This is our strongest territory and nobody local owns it yet, see [[Localization Playbook]]
- Tool walkthroughs, honestly, including where the tool is bad

This pillar is what earns the follow. It is also what makes someone trust us with a project.

### 2. Free assets

Templates, files, checklists, starter kits, components. Things people can take and use.

Highest saves and shares of anything we can post, and the most direct path from a follower to a lead, because using our template puts our standard inside their work. Also the easiest thing to gate later behind an email address if we ever want a list.

Candidates: a design system starter, an RTL checklist, a project scoping template, a brand guideline template, a proposal structure.

### 3. Behind the scenes

The team working. Explicitly requested and explicitly not corporate.

- Clips from real working sessions, screen and desk
- A problem being argued about, with the disagreement left in
- Setup, whiteboards, sticky notes, the ugly first version
- Small wins and small disasters
- The people, by name, with their own personalities intact

This pillar builds the closeness that turns followers into people who reach out. Nobody refers "a studio". They refer people they feel like they know.

### 4. Working in the open

What we are building right now, including [[Ventures]] while they are unfinished.

- Progress on a venture, screen by screen
- Decisions made and why, including the ones we reversed
- Numbers when we have them, once products are live
- Launches, and what happened after

Ventures and marketing feed each other here. The build is content, and the content is the venture's launch audience.

One caution: be deliberate about what is public before it exists, especially with client work under NDA, see [[Engagement Lifecycle]].

### 5. Opinion and market commentary

Where we say something. Positions on software quality in the Kingdom, on Arabic in products, on what local apps get wrong.

Highest risk, highest return. It is how a company becomes a name people quote rather than an account people follow. Keep it about the work, never about a named local company's failures. Punching down reads badly and travels further than anything good we make.

### Later, when it exists

**Case studies and client results.** Currently impossible, no delivered work. Will eventually be the strongest content for winning client projects, so write each one in the last week of the engagement, see [[Studio#Case studies]].

## Cadence

The rule is sustainability, not volume. Pick a cadence that survives a busy client week, because the alternative is a dead account with an apology on top.

Suggested starting point, to be agreed with the marketing lead rather than imposed:

| Frequency | What |
|---|---|
| 2 to 3 short posts a week | Craft content or behind the scenes, whichever exists that week |
| 1 substantial piece a month | A real breakdown, a free asset, or a proper build update |
| Continuous | Capture. Filming and screenshotting is a daily habit, not a production event |

Better to promise two a week and hold it for a year than five a week for a month.

## How it actually gets made

The production model matters more than the content calendar. A calendar full of ideas nobody has time to produce is the normal failure.

**Capture continuously, produce in batches.** Somebody films and screenshots while real work happens. That footage sits in a folder. Editing happens in one session a week, not per post.

**Raw beats polished for pillars 3 and 4.** A phone clip of a real screen at 11pm is better content than a designed graphic about our process. It is also faster to make, which is why it survives.

**One person owns publishing.** Content produced by a committee arrives late and sounds like nobody. See [[Roles]].

**Write in both languages from the start.** Not translated at the end. See [[Brand Voice#Arabic voice]].

## Surviving a busy client month

The single most important section in this note.

- **Keep a buffer.** Aim for two to four weeks of finished content scheduled ahead. Build the buffer when the studio is quiet, which is exactly when [[Business Lines#Capacity rule]] says to work on this
- **Client work is content.** With permission, the work we are being paid for feeds pillars 1, 3 and 4. That is the cheapest possible content, since it is already happening
- **Drop the cadence deliberately.** If a month has to be lighter, say so and pick a reduced rhythm on purpose. Silence with no explanation is what reads as abandoned
- **Never apologise for the gap in a post.** Just resume

## What we do not do

- Engagement bait, follow for follow, comment pods
- Reposting other people's content as our own insight
- Fake numbers, fake screenshots, mockups presented as shipped products. Same rule as [[Product Philosophy#Non negotiables]]
- AI generated slop as content. We can use AI to help make things, but the thing we publish has to be ours and has to be true
- Corporate announcements. See the banned phrases in [[Brand Voice]]

## Measurement

Saves, shares and DMs over likes and followers. The number that matters is how many real conversations came out of content, see [[Measurement#Marketing]].
