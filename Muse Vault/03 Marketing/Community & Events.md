# Community & Events

Workshops, streams, talks, courses, physical events. All of it is a maybe, deliberately. This note exists so the maybes have an order and a trigger rather than sitting as a vague someday.

## The ladder

Each step is cheap, tests demand, and earns the right to the next one. Same logic as [[Product Philosophy]]: do not build the expensive version of something until the cheap version showed signs of life.

| Step | Cost | What it tests | Trigger to try it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Free content | Low, already the plan | Does anyone care what we say | Now, see [[Content Engine]] |
| 2. Live stream or online session | Very low | Will people show up at a specific time | Once content is consistent and a topic keeps getting asked about |
| 3. Speak at someone else's event | Low, their audience and their logistics | Does the talk land in a room | Once we have one thing we can talk about with authority |
| 4. Our own small session, in person | Medium | Will people travel to us | Once a talk has worked twice and we know twenty people who would come |
| 5. Paid workshop or course | High to produce | Will people pay | Only after step 4 filled a room, and only if the audience research supports it |

Do not skip to step 5 because it is the one with revenue in it. A paid course with eleven signups costs more than it earns, in time and in how it looks.

## Fun formats

Not everything needs to be a lesson. Streams of the team working, building something live, a design review out loud, reacting to local apps. These sit close to the behind the scenes pillar in [[Content Engine]] and they build the closeness that makes people reach out.

Low production cost, high closeness, no obligation to be authoritative. Probably the best first experiment on the ladder.

## Speaking at other people's events

The highest return item on this list and the most overlooked, because it costs almost nothing. Someone else handles the venue, the audience and the promotion, and we get the recording as content forever.

- Tech and startup meetups, university sessions, accelerator programmes, community events
- Being on a stage is also how institutional relationships start, see [[Influence Strategy]]
- Always get the recording, and always publish it

To make this possible we need one talk we can give well. Pick a topic where we are genuinely the right people to speak: Arabic and RTL product quality is the obvious candidate.

## If we run a physical event

Notes for later, not now.

- Small and full beats large and empty. Thirty people in a room that holds thirty looks better than sixty in a hall for two hundred
- Riyadh venues, likely a partner space, an accelerator, or a cafe out of hours
- Gender mixed or segregated arrangements need deciding up front, since it affects the venue and who attends
- Prayer times structure the schedule, and Thursday evening or weekend timing needs thinking about, see [[Localization Playbook#Time and calendar]]
- Somebody films it. An event with no content coming out of it is a one day return on a month of work

## Courses

The most work of anything here and the easiest to underestimate. A course is a product, so if it ever happens it goes through [[Venture Pipeline]] like any other product rather than being treated as a marketing activity.

Real question to answer before starting: is there a course sized gap in Arabic for what we know. If the answer is yes and specific, it is interesting. If the answer is "we could teach design", it is not.
