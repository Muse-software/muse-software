# Marketing Lead Brief

For the conversation with the person who would take over marketing. Two halves: what to tell them, and what to ask them.

Name: to fill in. Add a note from [[Person]] in `08 People` after the conversation.

## The three minute version of Muse

Worth having this straight before the meeting, since how it is explained in the first five minutes is what they will repeat to other people.

Muse is a Saudi product company in Riyadh, at the very start. Three things at once:

1. **Our own apps.** We take products that already work outside the Kingdom, build the version that actually fits here, publish it, and put more behind whichever one takes off. Not reinventing anything. Careem did it with the Uber model, Jahez did it with delivery. One that works pays for all the ones that do not
2. **Brand and marketing, which is what we are talking to you about.** Building an audience through content that is genuinely useful and does not sound like a company. What that audience is for is further down
3. **Client work.** Product, design, development, branding and marketing for other businesses. This is where the money comes from at the start, and it funds the other two

Brand identity is finished. Website is in progress. Social accounts exist but nothing is on them yet. Nothing has been published, there is no audience, and there are no clients yet. That is the honest starting point and it is worth saying plainly, because someone joining now is building this from nothing rather than inheriting it.

## What the marketing pillar is for

The part that makes this more interesting than posting content. Four things, from [[Influence Strategy]]:

- **Clients who arrive already knowing us,** rather than us chasing work
- **A starting audience for our own apps,** so a launch is not shouted into an empty room
- **A way into the entities that back startups here,** ministries and large organisations, before we need anything from them
- **Public standing for everyone who works here,** including them. Anyone who works at Muse should leave more known than they arrived

## What we would be asking them to own

Written as an offer rather than a job description, since this is a conversation and the shape of it is partly theirs.

**Theirs to run**

- What we post, in what format, at what rhythm, see [[Content Engine]]
- The accounts themselves, starting with getting them activated properly, see [[Channels#Activation checklist]]
- The public voice, held consistently. Close to people, not corporate, see [[Brand Voice#The public voice]]
- Knowing who our audience actually is and checking it on a schedule, see [[Audience Research]]
- Community and events when the time comes, see [[Community & Events]]

**Shared**

- Deciding what the company says about itself. Positioning is Abdullah's, expressing it is theirs
- Client marketing and branding projects. When a client needs that work, they would be part of delivering it, and our own accounts go quieter that week. Worth being upfront about this, because it is a real feature of the job here and not an occasional exception
- Getting the rest of the team posting under their own names, since personal accounts carry more reach here than a logo does

**Not theirs**

- Product decisions and venture choices
- Sales, pricing and proposals
- The brand identity itself, which is done

## Already decided, so they are not walking into a blank page

Useful to name, because it shows the thinking is real and stops them wasting effort relitigating it.

- The identity is finished and is not being redesigned
- The voice is human and close to people. Not corporate, no announcements, no banned phrases, see [[Brand Voice]]
- Behind the scenes content of the team actually working is wanted, not just polished output
- Real things only. No invented numbers, no mockups presented as shipped work, no AI slop published as ours
- Arabic and English both matter, and both are written natively rather than translated
- Client work has first call on shared people, see [[Business Lines#Capacity rule]]

## Open, and genuinely theirs to shape

These are real questions, not tests with a right answer.

- Which two or three channels we start with, and which we ignore for now
- What cadence is actually sustainable. Better a small rhythm held for a year than a big one held for a month
- Which of the three audiences we build first: practitioners, buyers, or consumers. There is a recommendation in [[Audience Research#Three audiences, one account]] worth arguing about
- Whether free templates and assets are a main pillar or an occasional thing
- What the one thing is that we want to be known for

## A possible first thirty days

Offered as a starting shape, to be reworked with them rather than handed over.

**Week one.** Get the accounts activated. Logo, bios in both languages, handles consistent, contact route that someone watches. Read [[Marketing]] and its five notes.

**Week two.** Agree the channels, the cadence and the first content pillar. Set up however they want to capture and produce, see [[Content Engine#How it actually gets made]].

**Weeks three and four.** Publish. Build a small buffer of content so the first busy client week does not kill it. First few posts out, first sense of what lands.

No hard deadline attached to any of this. It is the order of operations, not a schedule.

## Questions worth asking them

Their answers matter more than anything above.

- What have they built an audience for before, and what actually worked
- Show me something you made that you are still proud of, and something that flopped
- How do you write in Arabic, and do you write it natively or translate
- What do you think of our brand, honestly, now that you have seen it
- Which local accounts do you think are doing this well, and why
- What would you not want to do
- What do you need from us to do this properly
- How much time can this realistically have, and what does that look like in a normal week

That last one matters more than it sounds. Marketing done at twenty percent of a person's attention still works if the cadence matches the attention. It fails when the plan assumes full time and the reality is not.

## What good looks like in six months

Deliberately modest, because the first six months of a content account are slow and pretending otherwise sets everyone up to feel like it failed.

- The accounts look alive and have not gone silent for a month
- Somebody outside our circle has reached out because of something we posted
- We know who our audience is well enough to describe them in a sentence
- The team is posting under their own names, not just the company account
- One repeatable format exists that is easy to keep making

Numbers to avoid celebrating: followers, likes, impressions. See [[Measurement#Marketing]].
