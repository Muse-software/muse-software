Brand and marketing

What marketing is actually for

This pillar earns no direct revenue, which is exactly why it has to be named as a pillar. Otherwise it becomes the thing that never happens the moment a client project lands.

What it buysWhat it looks like when it works
Inbound client workSomeone remembers Muse unprompted when their company needs product or design work. Cheaper than any sales process, and it lets us charge properly.
A launch audienceEvery product Muse builds needs people to publish to on day one, instead of shouting into an empty room.
Institutional accessBeing known to the entities that back startups here, ministries and large organisations, before we need anything from them.
Standing for the teamAnyone who works at Muse should leave more known and more employable than they arrived, whether they stay two years or ten.
The single most important instruction

Close to people, not corporate. If a caption could have been written by any company, rewrite it.

Big local firms all sound the same. Sounding like them makes Muse look smaller, not bigger. Muse says "we" and "I", people appear by name, posts show the messy middle and not only the finished thing, and there are no announcements about how excited we are to announce something. Humour is allowed. Forced humour is worse than none.

Personal accounts do the heavy lifting

In this market, personal accounts outperform a company account by a wide margin. A logo posting a tutorial gets a fraction of the reach the same tutorial gets from a named person. The model: the team posts as themselves, the company account amplifies and holds the archive, and nobody is required to post.

How content actually gets made

Five kinds of post. A healthy week has more than one kind in it.

PillarWhat it is
Useful craftTips, techniques, breakdowns. The thing someone saves and sends to a colleague. Muse's strongest unclaimed territory is Arabic and RTL specifics that almost everyone gets wrong.
Free assetsTemplates, checklists, starter kits. Highest saves and shares of anything postable, and the most direct path from a follower to a lead.
Behind the scenesThe team working, explicitly not corporate. A problem being argued about, with the disagreement left in.
Working in the openWhat Muse is building right now, including unfinished ventures. Numbers once products are live, and what happened after a launch.
Opinion and commentaryHighest risk, highest return. How a company becomes a name people quote rather than an account people follow. Never punches down at a named local company.
The production model

Capture continuously, produce in batches. Somebody films and screenshots while real work happens. Editing happens in one session, not per post. Raw beats polished for behind-the-scenes and working-in-the-open. One person owns publishing, since content by committee arrives late and sounds like nobody.

Surviving a busy client month

Keep a two to four week buffer of finished content, built when the studio is quiet. Client work is content too, with permission. Drop the cadence deliberately and say so, rather than going silent with no explanation. Never apologise for a gap, just resume.

06

How Muse measures marketing, in order

Same discipline as the product side. The flattering numbers are the ones platforms put on the front page. They are not the ones that matter.

#What matters
1Real conversations. How many people got in touch because of something posted. The only number that connects to revenue, and it is countable by hand.
2Saves and shares. Someone keeping a post or sending it to a colleague is worth far more than a like, and it is the signal algorithms actually reward.
3Repeat viewers. People coming back, the audience equivalent of retention.
4Audience composition. Who is actually following, checked quarterly. A smaller audience of the right people beats a bigger one of the wrong people.
5Consistency. Whether Muse published what it said it would. The most predictive number in year one, since almost every content account dies of stopping, not of bad content.
What not to celebrate

Followers, likes, impressions, reach. They move for reasons unrelated to anything you did, and optimising for them produces content that gets attention and no clients. No invented numbers, ever, in a deck, a pitch, or a post.

Comparing posts fairly

Raw totals favour whichever post got lucky reach. Compare rates instead: primary metric count per 1,000 people reached. A small post with a high save rate can beat a big post with a low one. The reusable post log in batch-01 already tracks this shape, ready for you to fill in as posts go out.

08

What good looks like, deliberately modest

The first stretch of a content account is slow. Pretending otherwise sets everyone up to feel like it failed.

  • The accounts look alive and have not gone silent for a month
  • Somebody outside Muse's circle has reached out because of something posted
  • Muse knows who its audience actually is well enough to describe them in a sentence
  • The team is posting under their own names, not only the company account
  • One repeatable format exists that is easy to keep making
The thing that kills studio content

Almost every studio account here follows the same arc: ten good posts, then a client project lands, then silence, then an apology post four months later. Keep a two to four week content buffer built when the studio is quiet, and drop the cadence deliberately with a stated reason rather than going silent. Never apologise for a gap, just resume.

Activation checklist, before publishing anything
  • Logo applied everywhere, correct variant per shape. Handles identical across platforms
  • Bio written natively in Arabic and English, no banned phrases
  • Contact route that someone actually monitors. An unanswered DM is worse than no DM channel
  • A pinned first post that introduces the company, written last

An account with no logo and no posts reads as abandoned, which is worse than not existing.
