# Marketing

Pillar two. Building an audience that knows who we are and what we are good at, then using that for the things in [[Influence Strategy]].

Owner: unassigned. A marketing lead conversation is the immediate next step, see [[Marketing Lead Brief]].

## Why this is a pillar and not a task

It earns nothing directly, so under any other structure it becomes the thing that never happens. Named as a pillar, it gets time on the bench by default, see [[Business Lines#Capacity rule]].

What it is actually for, in order of how soon it pays off:

1. **Inbound client work.** People remember us when their company, a friend's business, or their own vendor management needs this kind of work. That is cheaper than any sales process and it lets us charge properly
2. **A launch audience for [[Ventures]].** Publishing an MVP to nobody is the most expensive part of the venture model. An existing audience makes each attempt cheaper
3. **Institutional access.** Being known to the entities that support startups here, and to ministries and large organisations, before we need anything from them
4. **Standing for the people here.** Everyone at Muse should become more known by working here, see [[Positioning#Long term goal]]

## Repeatable batch system

The `AI Content Experiments` folder (`03 Marketing/AI Content Experiments/`) is a reusable workflow for producing source-backed social content batches. Batch 01 delivered six carousel/thread concepts with verified sources, posting playbooks, primary metrics, and a branded PDF. Future batches reuse the same structure instead of starting from scratch.

**New-session handoff:** Read [[AI Content Batch Production]] for the complete self-contained playbook. It tells a future agent exactly how to research new subjects, build a new batch, verify every claim, generate the Muse-branded PDF, and prepare the measurement log.

## The voice

Close to people, not corporate. See [[Brand Voice#The public voice]]. This is the single most important instruction in this folder and the easiest one to lose the moment someone writes a caption that sounds like a press release.

## The thing that kills studio content

Almost every studio account here follows the same arc. Ten good posts, then a client project lands, then silence, then an apology post four months later.

The mitigations are in [[Content Engine#Surviving a busy client month]]. They matter more than any single content idea in this folder.
