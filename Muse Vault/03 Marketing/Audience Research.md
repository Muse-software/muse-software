# Audience Research

Studying who is actually following us, on a schedule, so that when we have something to put in front of them we know what they can receive.

The point is not a report. It is being able to answer one question honestly at any time: if we published something tomorrow, who would see it and what would they do about it.

## Why bother

Because the opportunities will be varied. Our own tool or template, a venture launch, a workshop, someone else's product we agreed to promote, a hiring post, a partnership. Each of those needs a different audience, and guessing wrong burns credibility we cannot buy back.

An audience of designers will download a template and ignore a consumer app of ours. An audience of business owners will book a call and ignore a design tutorial. Both are valuable. They are not interchangeable.

## Three audiences, one account

Worth naming early, because it is the strategic decision hiding inside the marketing pillar.

| Audience | Who | What we would ever ask of them | Which pillar it serves |
|---|---|---|---|
| Practitioners and peers | Designers, developers, students, other studios | Follow, share, apply for a job, refer work | Marketing reach, hiring, some inbound |
| Buyers | Business owners, product and marketing managers, vendor management, institutions | Book a call, hire us, invite us in | [[Studio]] |
| Consumers | Ordinary people who would use whatever we publish | Download, subscribe, stay | [[Ventures]] |

One account cannot serve all three equally well. The content that earns practitioners is not what converts buyers, and neither builds a consumer app audience. It is also worth noting we do not yet know which category our products will be in, see [[Open Questions#4. Which category we start in]], which is another reason not to build a consumer audience on the company account yet.

**Recommendation, to argue with rather than accept.** Build the practitioner audience first and let the buyer audience come as a side effect. Reasons: craft content is what we can make today without any delivered client work, practitioners are also the hiring pool, buyers are heavily influenced by what practitioners say about a studio, and consumer venture launches will need paid acquisition and store optimisation anyway rather than organic brand following. Consumer audience gets built per product, not on the company account.

Record the decision in [[Decision Log]] once made, because it determines the content mix in [[Content Engine]].

## What to check every quarter

Twenty minutes with platform analytics, plus whatever we have learned from conversations. Not a research project.

**Who they are**

- Rough split of the three audiences above
- Cities. Riyadh, Jeddah, Dammam, outside the Kingdom
- Language they engage in, Arabic or English, and whether that differs by platform
- Seniority, as far as it is visible. Students and juniors behave very differently from decision makers

**What they respond to**

- Which posts got saved and shared, not which got likes
- Which content produced a DM or a real conversation
- Which content produced nothing, and whether we keep making it out of habit

**What they ask for**

- Recurring questions in comments and DMs. This is the most valuable input we get, since it is a content calendar and a product roadmap written by the audience
- Requests we keep declining. If three people ask for the same thing, note it

**What we can ask of them**

- Have we ever asked for anything, and what happened
- Would this audience pay for something. A course, a workshop, a template pack
- Do we know anyone in this audience by name yet. Ten real relationships beat ten thousand followers

## Methods, cheapest first

1. **Platform analytics.** Free, immediate, and enough for most of the above
2. **Ask them directly.** Polls, question stickers, and the open question in a post. People here answer generously when it does not feel like market research
3. **Read the DMs properly.** The highest quality signal we will get, and it is already sitting there
4. **A short survey.** Worth doing once the following is large enough that the responses mean something. Five questions maximum
5. **Talk to ten of them.** Once a year, actual conversations with people who follow us. Nothing else tells you why

## Rules

- **No fabricated numbers, ever.** Not in a deck, not in a pitch, not in a post. Same rule as [[Product Philosophy#Non negotiables]]
- **Report the number that matters, not the flattering one.** Followers are the flattering one, see [[Measurement#Marketing]]
- **If we promote something for someone else, we are spending trust.** Only for things we would use. A studio that promotes anything is worth nothing to promote through
