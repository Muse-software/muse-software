# Experiment 1. Three model launches in one month, and how to actually pick one

## 1. Premise and audience job

In one month, three labs shipped major model updates: Claude Opus 5, OpenAI's GPT-5.6 family, and Google's Gemini Flash trio [1][2][3]. Each gives you a different reason to switch. The job for the reader: stop comparing vendor pitches and pick a model the way you would pick a contractor, on your own work, not someone else's slide.

## 2. Best format and channel adaptation

X carousel as the primary format, mirrored as a 5 slide Instagram carousel. The X thread is the same argument broken into short posts, see `threads/exp-1-thread.md`. LinkedIn gets the same substance with the routing checklist pulled to the top, posted a day or two after the X and Instagram versions once early replies have shaped the framing.

## 3. Truthful hook

"Three major AI releases landed in one month. Each gives you a different reason to switch."

## 4. On-slide copy, 5 slides

**Slide 1**
Three major AI releases landed in one month.
Each gives you a different reason to switch.

**Slide 2**
July 2026: Claude Opus 5 [1], OpenAI's GPT-5.6 family, Sol, Terra, Luna [2], and Google's Gemini 3.6 Flash, 3.5 Flash-Lite, and 3.5 Flash Cyber [3].
Each launch sells a different win. None of those vendor claims measures your work.

**Slide 3**
Read the fine print. Anthropic calls Opus 5 its new default on Claude Max, at $5 input and $25 output per million tokens [1]. OpenAI says Sol sets a new state of the art on its own benchmark, at less than half the tokens of the model it is compared against [2]. Google is explicit that its Flash trio is not even its flagship line, that is still Gemini Pro [3].
Three different companies, three different definitions of winning.

**Slide 4**
So pick for your work, not the leaderboard: your task, your language including Arabic, your latency and cost, tested on your own examples, not the vendor's.

**Slide 5**
Which model actually holds up on your real work, and what do you route where?

## 6. Caption or opener

Opus 5, GPT-5.6, and a new Gemini Flash trio all landed within a few weeks of each other. Each gives you a different reason to switch. Here is how we pick without treating any vendor pitch as our own evaluation.

## 7. Discussion question

Which model actually holds up on your real work, and what do you route where?

## 8. Why it can earn engagement, by behaviour

- Comments: people naming their actual default model and what they route to it, which is a low-effort, high-signal reply to leave
- Saves: a reusable model-picking heuristic that outlasts this month's launches
- Shares: sent into a team channel mid-argument about which model to standardise on
- Quote posts: practitioners adding their own selection rule or contradicting the routing advice with their own setup

## 9. Risks and how the published version avoids them

- News goes stale fast. Publish within days of drafting, and the copy names the month, not "this week," so it still reads correctly later.
- Restating vendor benchmark numbers as settled fact would mislead. Every number here is attributed to the company that published it, and slide 3 says outright that the three companies do not even agree on what counts as flagship.
- Reading as an ad for any one lab. The copy names all three, criticises all three equally for reporting only their own comparisons, and the punchline is to distrust all of them equally.

## 10. Distribution plan

Personal account first: the founder who owns model tooling and routing decisions posts from their own account. Muse amplifies within a few hours with a one line framing and keeps the archive copy. LinkedIn adaptation: same substance, posted a day or two later, leads with the routing checklist as a practical takeaway for teams standardising on a model, keeps the closing question.

## 11. Founder first-day interaction plan

- Before posting: reply usefully to 3 to 5 recent posts from people discussing model choice or routing, no link drops
- First 60 to 90 minutes after posting: answer every substantive comment with a fact or a follow-up question, not a thank-you; deepen the single best comment with a real reply
- Quote-post targets: 1 to 2 credible model-evaluation or developer-tools accounts who posted on the same launches recently, quoted to extend the discussion, never to dunk
- Follow-up: collect the routing choices people share in replies into a short follow-up post or a shared note, credited to whoever contributed

## 12. Success hypothesis and metrics

Hypothesis: a practical, vendor-neutral picking guide dropped during a noisy launch month earns saves, because it is useful past the news cycle. Primary metric: saves. Secondary diagnostics: substantive replies naming real routing choices, plus reach and impressions tracked but not celebrated on their own.
