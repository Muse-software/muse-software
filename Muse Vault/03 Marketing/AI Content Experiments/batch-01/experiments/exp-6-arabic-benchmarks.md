# Experiment 6. The Arabic benchmarks everyone cites have measurable errors

## 1. Premise and audience job

A 2026 quality-first Arabic leaderboard found systematic errors in benchmarks the field already treats as settled, and showed that a small Arabic-specialised model can beat a much larger multilingual one on specific domains [13][14]. This is Muse's native-quality thesis with evidence behind it. The job for the reader, GCC builders and Arabic-NLP practitioners especially: stop picking a model off a leaderboard average, and test on your own Arabic.

## 2. Best format and channel adaptation

Instagram carousel as the primary format, this is the RTL showcase so the Arabic pass matters most here, paired with an X post. LinkedIn speaks to institutions and buyers choosing Arabic AI, leaning on Muse's native-quality position.

## 3. Truthful hook

"The Arabic benchmarks everyone cites have measurable errors. And a small Arabic-first model can beat a giant one."

## 4. On-slide copy, 5 slides

**Slide 1**
We rank Arabic models off leaderboard averages.
What if the benchmarks themselves are dirty?

**Slide 2**
QIMMA <span class="ar">قِمّة</span>, built by TII in April 2026, quality-checked popular Arabic benchmarks before scoring anything, and found systematic issues [13]. On ArabicMMLU alone, it discarded 436 of 14,163 items as flawed, a 3.1% discard rate [13][14].

**Slide 3**
Scale did not decide the ranking. Some smaller Arabic-specialised models, named in the same leaderboard as Fanar-1-9B and ALLaM-7B, outperform much larger multilingual models on specific domains [13].

**Slide 4**
Why it matters here: Arabic is spoken by over 400 million people and still makes up only about 0.5% of web data [15]. Native quality is a choice, not a default. Test on your own Arabic and dialects, not an average.

**Slide 5**
Where has a smaller Arabic-first model beaten a bigger one for you, and which Arabic tasks still break?

## 9. Risks and how the published version avoids them

- Framing this as an attack on any Saudi or regional model would be unfair and inaccurate. The copy presents TII's method and finding, not a ranking of rivals, and TII itself ships a competing model, which is disclosed in the source register.
- QIMMA is one team's leaderboard, method, and rankings, not independently corroborated. Stated plainly in the source register rather than presented as consensus.
- The lead source is about two weeks past the 90 day window. Disclosed openly rather than hidden.
- The Arabic run on slide 2 is a single verified word, the project's own name as it appears on the source page, not a machine-translated line. No other Arabic appears in this carousel.
