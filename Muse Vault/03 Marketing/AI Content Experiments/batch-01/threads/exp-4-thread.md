# X thread, Experiment 4. AI trust

1/ We trust algorithms too much. Then one mistake makes us trust them too little.

2/ A 2026 study: 58% of participants said AI "did most of the thinking" on their task. Those participants reported less confidence in their own reasoning and less ownership of the work [28].

3/ People who challenged or modified AI suggestions reported stronger confidence and authorship [28]. Passive acceptance and active use are not the same behaviour.

4/ Algorithm appreciation, 2019: people followed advice more when they believed it came from an algorithm than when they believed it came from a person [23].

5/ Algorithm aversion, 2015: after seeing the same mistake, people lost confidence in an algorithm faster than in a human, even when the algorithm had performed better [22].

6/ These older studies are pre-LLM behavioural concepts, not proof about current chatbots. But the tension is useful: defer too easily, then overreact to one failure.

7/ Where do you switch from trusting AI to rejecting it, and what evidence moves that line?

Sources: [28] Tech Xplore, 2026-04-16. [22] Dietvorst, Simmons, Massey, 2015. [23] Logg, Minson, Moore, 2019. Full register in sources/sources.md.
