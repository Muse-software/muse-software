Why the proof wall works

The research on work-led companies (Stripe, Vercel, Linear, Framer) produced five mechanics, and each one is present in this wall.

MechanicSourceHow it appears here
Proof as stories, not adjectivesStripeEvery card carries one concrete number or fact: 200k transactions, 12 minutes, 94% accuracy, 500k users.
Never a metric without a nameStripeEach metric is tied to a named, visitable destination: a case study page or a live URL.
Live, playable workFramerMuse Experiments and Tenet are live links, not screenshots. A visitor can click through and use the work.
Recently shippedVercelThe wall mixes shipped client work with in-progress ventures, so the site grows as the work grows.
Engagement, not testimonialsLinearNo quotes, no stars, no "trusted by". The work and its results carry the credibility.
The "portfolio as we go" problem

Muse is still early; a full case study library does not exist yet. The wall answers this honestly: three anonymized case studies of real shipped work, two live ventures with real URLs, one content line. As new work ships, new cards are added. Nothing on the wall is fabricated, and nothing is a placeholder for work that does not exist.

What the wall deliberately avoids

Fake testimonials. Vague claims ("we transform businesses"). Consultant vocabulary ("synergy", "digital transformation"). Stock team photos. "Learn more" links that go nowhere. Every card has a real destination or it does not ship.

Structure and sourcing

The order of the page follows the research findings from a study of work-led companies, adapted to Muse's reality. The recommended section order was: hero with proof promise, proof wall, live experiments, how we build, community, credibility anchors, CTA. 08

Open decisions

Six decisions remain. Each carries a recommendation; a different answer from the team changes the plan.

1. Which case studies are real?

Three anonymized cards exist: core banking, underwriting, super-app redesign. They describe real engagements from the founding experience, anonymized by requirement. The team must confirm each card is a true account of shipped work before launch. A fabricated card would kill the direction's credibility.

2. Live URLs for the ventures

The wall links to experiments.muse.sa and tenet.muse.sa. Those domains and products must be live (or staged with real content) before the site launches. A "View live" link that 404s is worse than no link.

3. Arabic proof wall

The hero, doors, and closing are fully translated, but the proof wall cards fall back to English until the native Arabic writer reviews the summaries. Recommendation: ship English-first per the bilingual plan, translate in phase 2.

4. Metrics that invite scrutiny

"200k daily transactions", "94% accuracy", "40% retention lift": each is a claim a buyer will test. The case study pages must carry the evidence for each number, or the number should be softened. Recommendation: keep the numbers, build the evidence pages.

5. Proof wall placement

The wall sits immediately after the hero, before the doors. This is the direction's whole point: work first. If the team finds the wall too aggressive before the pitch, it can move below the doors without changing any copy.

6. Field Notes as a proof card

The newsletter is content, not shipped work. It earns a place as the third category because it shows the work in progress, the same way Linear's changelog does. Recommendation: keep it, but make sure the newsletter actually ships weekly before launch.
