## Summary of Mechanics Worth Borrowing

1. **"Your X. Your Y. Your Z." trio framing** (Salla EN: "Your store. Your brand. Your future.") — Three possessive "Your" lines create instant parallelism for Muse's three doors without needing to name the doors explicitly. Works in both EN and AR.

2. **Title + subtitle + body 3-line card with consistent rhythm** (Salla, ZTO, Mozn) — Every card follows the pattern: 1–3 word title, 3–6 word subtitle, 8–20 word body. The subtitle acts as a "promise" and the body as a "proof point." This rhythm keeps cards visually balanced and avoids eyebrow-heavy design.

3. **Contrastive positioning in client-facing copy** (ZTO Partner: "adds to your credibility rather than dilute it" / "not the other way around") — The strongest example of a company distinguishing its own product/values from client work. The "rather than" and "not the other way around" structures create a clear boundary between what the company does for clients vs. what it stands for itself.

4. **Bilingual parallelism with shared metrics** (ZTO: "100K decision-makers" in both EN and AR; Lean: "make tomorrow's healthcare a reality" / "تجعل المستقبل واقعًا") — When the same metric or phrase appears in both languages at the same grid position, it creates cross-language consistency that signals a unified brand, not a translated one.
