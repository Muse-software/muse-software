Two languages, one site

Both locales carry the same sections and the same keys; nothing is missing in one and present in the other. The English and Arabic are written independently, so register and sentence length differ on purpose. The door cards are bilingual on the page: title, body, promise and CTA all render in the active language. No eyebrow labels; the title carries the lane.

Revision 2 (2026-08-05)

The doors were rebuilt after review: per-card and section eyebrows removed (Linear, Vercel and Stripe all open lanes with a title, no kicker), the Tenex hero line replaced with the company identity, and every door line re-sourced from live references. The em-dash and emoji sweep is applied across both languages.

The objections, answered

Five questions, each one an objection a serious buyer raises before committing. The FAQ trust block is the same pattern tenex.co uses: answer the objection on the page, in the buyer's language.

English
How is Muse different from a typical dev shop?
"We're a small, senior team, not a rotating cast of contractors. No 100-slide strategy decks with nothing behind them. We scope fast, build with you, and measure ourselves on what actually shipped."
What does AI integration actually mean?
"Finding where AI removes real friction in how your business runs, building the systems that do it reliably, and getting your team actually using them. Then repeating that cycle as the tools improve."
How does pricing work?
"Every engagement is scoped and priced on its own, once we understand what you're actually trying to do. Tell us the problem and we'll come back with a number and exactly what sits inside it."
Who do you typically work with?
"Teams that want to move fast without dropping the quality bar. That runs from a founder shipping a first product to an enterprise team trying to get an AI initiative out of pilot purgatory."

09

Open decisions

Four decisions remain. Each carries a recommendation; a different answer from the team changes the plan.

1. Doors as navigation, or doors as sections?

The three doors sit mid-page and link onward. They could also become the primary navigation, with /explore retired. Recommendation: keep the doors as homepage sections and keep /explore as the deep-dive; a buyer who wants one door gets the full page, a buyer who wants all three gets the summary.

2. The Think door, before the content exists

The Think door promises articles, tutorials and a newsletter. The newsletter signup is live; the articles are not published yet. Recommendation: ship the door with the newsletter linked, and hold the articles until the first two are written. A door that links to nothing erodes trust faster than a door that is missing.

3. The Arabic register

The doors copy is deliberately colloquial ("نشتغل بها", "شوف اللي نطلقه") while the legal and about pages stay formal. Recommendation: confirm the split with a native copy review before /ar ships; colloquial for hero, doors and FAQ, formal for legal and careers.

4. Unused service entries in the message files

The content files carry three service entries under the doors section (product-engineering, ai-integration, brand-interface) that no page renders. Recommendation: remove them before merge so the content files match what the site actually shows.
