Site map

The site is nine routes, ordered the way a stranger meets us. Each page has one job. This is also the build order: the landing first, everything else hangs off it.

No Work page yet

The site deliberately does not have a case-studies page. We will not claim client work we cannot show. It goes live the day the first shipped product can be put on display — until then, the proof is the product itself.

The landing

One page, one job: turn a stranger into a lead. It opens on the hero and walks down through what we do, who it's for, and why now — ending in a single ask. Eight sections, in order.

S1 Hero
  • Full-bleed textured background. Two lines: "Everyone has an AI strategy." / "Almost nobody has shipped one." The second line carries a marker highlight on load.
  • Below: "We're a product team in Riyadh." and the kicker "We actually build the thing, not the deck about the thing."
  • CTA "Get in touch" — on desktop an arrow points at it. Click anywhere: the texture blasts into pixels.
S2 Approach

Eyebrow "our approach", title "Muse helps you shift from AI-absent to AI-native." Three cards: AI Transformation, Product Engineering, Gamification & Experience. Each card carries its own verb CTA.

S3 Thesis

"The next decade of software gets decided in the next two years." The site's thesis, stated once, plainly.

S4 Who we build for — from zto.sa

"aren't a general audience." Two audiences: founders shipping a first product; operators pulling an AI initiative out of pilot purgatory. The role nouns get the highlight sweep on scroll-in.

S5 Outcomes band — from mozn.ai

One line, verbatim: "We deliver outcomes, not just tools." Large, centered, no hover.

S6 Marquee

A ticker strip. Content decision: drop the claim-y "trusted by leaders" — the honest option is "Nothing ships that we wouldn't use ourselves."

S7 FAQ

"Questions? We have answers." Five items. Accordion, one open at a time.

S8 Final CTA

"Tell us what you're trying to build." / "The first conversation is with the people who would build it, not a sales layer." / "Get started".

Borrowed, flagged

Two-audience line from salla: "Built for dreamers. Trusted by sellers." becomes "Built for founders. Trusted by operators." The "trusted" half is a claim. Ship "Built for founders. Built for operators." until we have a client to stand behind it.

Services and explore

The landing says what we do in three cards. Explore and the service pages say it fully. All four share one arc, borrowed from mozn's solution pages: hero, why you need us, what we do, approach, why work with us, FAQ, CTA.

Explore — the services overview

Three cards, full. Each carries a summary and a two-tier CTA, a pattern from tenex: "Learn more" to the service page, "Get started" to intake. The verbs vary per service — that is the discipline salla and tenex both follow.

  • AI Transformation. "Your AI strategy, executed all the way to production, not just decked." CTA "Plan the transformation".
  • Product Engineering. "Production-grade software, shipped fast, without dropping the quality bar." CTA "Build the product".
  • Gamification & Experience. "Turn adoption into something people actually want to do." CTA "Design the experience".
What the service pages borrow
ServiceBorrowed line

Product Engineering"We work inside your team, not from a distance." (adapted from mozn.ai)
GamificationNo borrow needed. Muse-specific and strong, keep it
On every service page

  • Sticky sub-nav on desktop: Overview / Why / What we do / FAQ. Collapses to a pill row on mobile.

About

About answers the three questions a stranger has: who are you, why should I believe you, why now. The structure follows zto's about page: hero, the moment, who we build for, how we work, team, careers teaser.

S1 Hero

"Riyadh-built. Globally standard." / "A product company working with businesses that want it built properly." Arabic: "صُنع في الرياض. بمعيار عالمي."

We do not mint a category-owning line. Lean owns "The digital enabler of healthcare in Saudi Arabia" because it earned it. We have not earned a category yet.
S2 Why Muse — the moment — borrowed from zto

Saudi Arabia's economy is going through a moment that doesn't repeat itself. Companies are being built from scratch, and a generation of founders and operators is building something genuinely new.

The first two sentences are zto.sa verbatim. The last two are ours: "Most of what gets shipped still isn't built to a standard that lasts. That's the gap Muse was built for."

S3-S7 — the rest, in order
  • Who we build for. The same zto-borrowed line as the landing, written once in the codebase and rendered on both pages.
  • How we work. Tabs: About / Mission / Vision / Values, plus a beliefs carousel. Tabs switch instantly; the carousel has prev/next; on mobile it becomes a horizontal scroll or accordion.
  • Team. Two named founders. We do not claim a team size or roles we cannot field.
  • Careers teaser. "We're a small team, growing carefully." + "View careers".
  • Ticker. "Nothing ships that we wouldn't use ourselves."
Arabic, flagged for the reviewer

"يمرّ الاقتصاد السعودي بمرحلة نوعية لم تتكرر مسبقًا" is zto verbatim and strong. The Muse continuation carries "لسه" and "مو" as spoken markers; confirm it lands as talking, not performing.

Careers and contact

Careers

The page borrows tenex's human fallback, salla's origin line, mozn's "join the team building X". Careers copy runs in the spoken register.

  • Hero. "Come build with us." / "We're a small, senior team based in Riyadh. Reach out even if nothing below is an exact match."
  • Origin line — from salla. "من قلب الرياض إلى العالم" adapted from salla's "من قلب مكة إلى العالم". A hero kicker, with the stretched letter-spacing on العالم like salla does.
  • Why join — from mozn. "Join the team building products people here actually use." Not mozn's "AI rocketship" or headcount lines.
  • Claim line — from salla. "نبحث عن الأفضل" verbatim. MSA spine, works as a heading.
  • Human fallback — from tenex. "No exact match? Say hi anyway." / "ما لقيت اللي يناسبك؟ سلّم علينا على أي حال."
Contact

The page borrows zto's second-person conditional CTA. Deliberately not mozn's two-tier "Get in touch / Contact sales" — the whole pitch is no sales layer.

  • Hero. "Let's talk." / "Tell us what you're building. We usually reply the same day."
  • Conditional line — from zto. "If you're looking for a team to actually build the thing, not just deck it, talk to us." Adapted from zto's "إذا كنت تبحث عن شريك لتعزيز سردية شركتك وتمييزها في السوق، تواصل معنا".
  • Methods. WhatsApp primary (per the localization playbook), email, socials. Consider hello@muse.sa over info@.
  • Form. Budget ranges are collected privately — different from publishing pricing, and allowed.

Newsletter and footer

Newsletter

The richest borrow here is thmanyah's "تغنيك عن" value-prop. The daily-coffee framing does not fit — we send when there is something worth saying, not daily. Borrow the construction, not the ritual.

  • Hero. "Get the field notes in your inbox." / "The same thinking that goes into the work, sent directly. No need to check back."
  • AR refinement — from thmanyah. "تغنيك عن متابعة أخبار الذكاء الاصطناعي العامة، ونختار لك اللي يستحق." Adapted from thmanyah's "تغنيك عن التصفّح العشوائي لشبكات التواصل".
  • The "زائد:" device — from thmanyah. Every thmanyah title carries a secondary hook: "زائد:". Use it in future issue titles as the kicker before a second angle. EN equivalent: "Plus:".
  • Name it. The newsletter has no name. thmanyah names its products evocatively: "بين الخطوط", "مصدر مطّلع". Candidates: "بين الأنظمة" (AR) + "Field Notes" (EN). Or stay unnamed.
  • Do not claim "best". thmanyah owns "أفضل محتوى عربي على الإنترنت". We cannot claim a superlative yet.
Footer — global
  • Keep. Blurb + "Get started" + "Reach out to us" + Riyadh location + Company/Explore columns + rights line + Privacy / Terms.
  • Worldview line — tenex mechanic, our line. tenex closes with "Stay on the right side of history." Ours, reused from the hero: "We build the thing, not the deck about the thing."
  • Phone / Complaints — salla GCC norm. Only if a real number and intake exist. A dead Complaints link reads worse than none.
The one rule that keeps us honest

Every borrowed line in this document is either verbatim, a marked adaptation with the original shown, or Muse's own. Nothing is invented. Lines that make a claim we have not earned are marked EMPTY until real: proof stats, superlatives, "trusted by", category-owning "the".

Interactions and mobile

The interactions are where the site becomes Muse. A few rules, applied everywhere, so it does not feel like a collection of pages.

Dither hero
  • Click (desktop) / tap (mobile) triggers the pixel-blast. Mobile uses a single blast with a reduced particle budget for frame rate.
  • Reduced-motion: dither renders as a static textured still, blast disabled, no auto-animation. Hard requirement, not optional.
Highlight effect

White background / black text marker sweep on the two hero lines. Fires on load, and on scroll-into-view for the role-noun reveals, once. Never re-triggers on re-scroll, to avoid a flickering page on mobile.

Hover states
  • Cards: raise + shadow + the per-service verb CTA underline draws in. Image cards get a subtle parallax on desktop only.
  • Links: underline draws from the leading edge. In RTL that edge flips to the right.
  • CTAs: primary button fills/darkens. The arrow at the hero CTA is desktop only.
RTL mirroring — the easy place to break
  • Full layout mirror: nav, chevrons, carousels, progress, back gestures, sliders.
  • The arrow points at the CTA on desktop; in RTL it mirrors so it still aims at the button, not away. A naive mirror that points it off-screen is the bug to avoid.
  • Marquee direction flips in Arabic.
  • Mixed-direction strings (Latin "Muse" inside Arabic sentences) are where RTL bugs concentrate. Test every one.
  • Arabic runs shorter but taller. Line-height needs its own pass, not just a width mirror.
  • Numerals stay Western (0-9) in Arabic.
Touch / mobile
  • No hover-reveal anywhere. Anything gated behind hover must be visible by default on touch: verb CTAs, values copy, role details.
  • Filter pill rows and sub-navs become horizontal scrollers or collapse.
  • Accordions for FAQ and About tabs.
  • Arrow on the hero CTA: hidden on mobile, both directions.
Reduced motion, everywhere

Dither static, marquee static, highlight instant (no sweep), card parallax off, arrow hidden. Honor prefers-reduced-motion on every page, not just the hero.

Open decisions

Ten calls to make. Each with a recommendation. Most are reversible, none are urgent, all shape the voice. A different answer from the team changes the plan.

Build order
  • 1. Landing: hero + interactions first, then the eight sections in order.
  • 2. Explore: three cards, two-tier CTAs.
  • 3. Service pages: shared arc, per-service FAQ.
  • 4. About: moment framing, tabs, team.
  • 5. Careers: origin line, why-join, claim line, fallback.
  • 6. Contact / Get started: conditional CTA, email decision.
  • 7. Newsletter: "تغنيك عن" sub, name decision.
  • 8. Footer + legal pages.
  • 9. Hover, RTL, and mobile pass end to end.
  • 10. Native Arabic review before anything ships on /ar.
